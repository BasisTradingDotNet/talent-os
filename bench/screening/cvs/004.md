I have spent the last two and a half years trading crypto derivatives in Dubai: first as a Derivatives Trader on the crypto desk of Falconridge Brokerage DMCC (March 2024 to June 2025), and since July 2025 as a Quant Trader at Dunecrest Capital Management, a small fund running basis and funding strategies across Binance, Bybit, OKX and Deribit. On both desks I have traded as one of a small team inside the desk's book rather than running a book of my own; what I have owned is the margin model and the risk framework.

The work I know best is the mechanics of perpetuals and dated futures across venues. At Dunecrest I built the cross-venue margin model the desk uses to decide where each leg should sit. It takes each venue's maintenance-margin ladder (tiered by position notional on Binance and Bybit, portfolio-based on Deribit), the collateral haircuts and each venue's mark-price method, and produces a liquidation price for every position and a distance to liquidation for every account under a set of shocks (spot down 15%, basis widening by three points, funding flipping sign). Funding settles every eight hours on the main venues and hourly on some newer contracts, so the model also projects the next three settlements' cash flows, and collateral is moved before it is needed rather than after. I quote basis in annualised terms: a quarterly trading 2.1% above spot with 80 days to expiry is a 9.6% annualised carry before funding and fees, and the desk's entry rule is net carry above 7% annualised with a positive funding forecast.

I owned the risk framework at Dunecrest. I designed the position and exposure limits (per-venue notional, net delta, and a cap on the share of collateral held at any one venue), the drawdown ladder that cuts size at 2% and stops trading at 4% of desk capital, and the pre-trade checks that enforce all of it in the order path. I wrote the reconciliation checks that run at every funding settlement, comparing internal positions and balances against every venue, and the daily risk report that the fund's two partners read each morning. Over fourteen months the desk has had zero limit breaches and two reconciliation breaks, both caught by the checks the same day and closed within the hour.

My tools are Python. The margin model, the reconciliation checks and the daily report run as one scheduled pipeline, dc-margin, built on pandas and PostgreSQL, deployed with cron on the desk server, with pytest tests covering the margin ladders and the settlement projection. The same pipeline produces the morning PnL and margin summary that the desk trades from each day.
