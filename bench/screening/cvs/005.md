## Projects

### lmn-platform — Python trading platform (owner, 2024 – present)

The desk's core system: venue adapters for Binance, Bybit, OKX and Deribit over REST and websocket, an order manager with hedge-leg pairing and retry logic, a fills and positions ledger on PostgreSQL, and a scheduler for the desk's daily jobs. About 28,000 lines of Python with roughly 1,100 pytest tests, type-checked with mypy, packaged as internal wheels and deployed with Docker Compose. I am the owner: I review every change, run the release process, and take the first on-call slot each week. It has handled every order the desk has sent since launch; unplanned downtime in 2025 was under four hours in total, all of it written up.

### lmn-recon — reconciliation (owner, 2024 – present)

Compares ledger fills, positions and balances against each venue's trade history and account endpoints at every funding settlement and at end of day, and posts breaks to the desk channel with the affected account and the size of the difference. Used daily by the desk, the ops lead and the finance team. Breaks fell from around ten a week in the first month (mostly fee rounding, plus one duplicate-fill bug in an adapter that the tool itself exposed) to fewer than one a week, and the finance close moved from the afternoon of T+1 to the evening of T+0.

### lmn-tca — execution analytics (owner, 2025 – present)

Slippage against arrival mid per venue, order type and size bucket; fill ratio; time to fill; and realised against predicted funding per position. A nightly job with a Grafana dashboard and a Monday summary that the desk reads before the weekly meeting. Findings so far: moving ETH hedge legs from market to post-only orders on one venue cut average slippage on that leg by about 2 bp, and one venue's fill ratio on small orders was poor enough that it was dropped for that size bucket.

### lmn-funding — funding tracker (owner, 2024 – present)

Collects predicted and realised funding, mark and index prices and open interest for every contract the desk trades, every minute, into PostgreSQL; the desk's morning view of where funding sits across venues comes from this. Alerts when the spread between venues widens past a configured level or when predicted and realised funding diverge. It is a data and alerting tool; entry and exit decisions stay with the traders.

### lmn-reports — end-of-day reporting and automation (owner, 2024 – present)

Automated end-of-day PnL, position and funding report, generated nightly and delivered by email and to the desk channel; it replaced a spreadsheet process that took the ops lead about two hours a day. Also runs the desk's morning checklist automations: venue status, collateral balances, futures approaching expiry. Has a failure alert and a runbook; it has missed its 07:00 delivery three times in two years, each time written up.

### gw-session — Rust component of the order gateway (2025)

The desk's order gateway was rewritten in Rust by a colleague in 2025, and I shipped its session module: connection lifecycle, heartbeat and reconnect handling, credential rotation and message framing for the four venues, with unit tests and a small integration harness against the venues' testnets. It has been in production since October 2025. It was my first Rust in production; I keep a personal Rust project (a funding-history command-line tool) going to stay in practice.

### lmn-alerts — alerting bot (owner, 2024 – present)

A small Python service that takes alerts from the platform, the funding tracker and the reconciliation job, de-duplicates them and routes them to Slack and to the on-call phone by severity. Used by the whole desk; it is how the desk learned about the June 2025 venue outage before the venue's own status page changed.

### Backtest harness (2024)

A Python harness for funding-capture and calendar-carry strategies on recorded data, used for sizing rules and for the desk's weekly review. Not a production system, and deliberately kept separate from the platform code.
