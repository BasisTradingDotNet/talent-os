I write about crypto markets for a living. For the past three and a half years I have run an independent newsletter and video channel, Perp Notes, that explains how crypto markets work to a paying audience of around 4,000 subscribers. Before that I worked for two years as a content editor at Fennimore Media, a small trade-publishing house in Bristol, where I covered fintech and payments. I have never worked on a trading desk; what I bring is the ability to explain complicated mechanics clearly, and a genuine obsession with the plumbing of perpetual futures markets.

The work I am proudest of is the explainer series. "Funding rates, from first principles" walks through how a perpetual's funding payment is computed from the gap between the perp price and the index price, and who pays whom. "Cash-and-carry in practice" follows a worked example of buying spot and selling the quarterly future on Deribit and holding to expiry, and shows where the basis comes from and how it closes. "Why did I get liquidated?" explains why the liquidation price on a cross-margin account moves when other positions gain or lose, and why the mark price rather than the last trade is what triggers it. Each piece is checked against the exchange documentation and against my own small positions on Bybit and Binance, which I have traded with personal money since 2022.

I am not an engineer, but I know enough Python to be useful: the charts in my articles come from notebooks where I pull funding and open-interest history from exchange APIs and plot it. I have not built anything that runs on its own or that anybody else uses. I am careful with money — I keep my personal trading small and treat risk management as a discipline — but I have no professional risk experience to point to.

I hold a BA in Economics from the University of Corvedale (2019). I am applying because I want to move from explaining the basis trade to working on it, and I am prepared to start in a junior seat and learn the engineering side properly.
