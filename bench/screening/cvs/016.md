I have spent the last four years as a discretionary futures trader at Ravenhurst Trading LLP, a proprietary trading firm in London, from September 2022 to the present. I run my own book: index futures on CME and Eurex, and for the last two and a half years crypto perpetuals and quarterly futures on Binance, Bybit and Deribit. The book is mine end to end, from idea to execution to sign-off with the risk manager at the close, and my pay has been a share of its PnL since the first year. Before Ravenhurst I spent three years at a small spread-betting shop, Alderley Brokers, on a client-facing desk, which taught me how retail flow behaves but is not what I would call real trading experience.

Most of my crypto trading is around funding and carry. I track the funding rate on the major perps across Binance and Bybit and watch the spread between the quarterly future and the perp; when funding has been persistently paying one way for a few sessions I put on the carry, hedged on the quarterly or in spot, and unwind it when the spread closes. I keep a running log of every funding payment received or paid on each position so I know what the carry has actually earned rather than what it looked like on the screen.

Risk is the part of the job I take most seriously. My book has a hard daily loss limit of 1.5% of allocated capital and a weekly limit of 4%; if either is hit I am flat and off the desk for the rest of the period, no exceptions. I halve position size after a 6% drawdown from the equity high-water mark and only scale back up after a full month above the previous high. Every position has a maximum size per venue agreed with the risk manager, and I do not hold a crypto position on a single venue that I could not close inside ten minutes in a stressed market. I have sat through two exchange outages with open positions and both times the pre-agreed venue caps kept the damage to a bad day rather than a bad month.

I am not a programmer. I use Python in Jupyter notebooks to look at my own fills and funding history, mostly pandas and matplotlib. Nothing I have written runs anywhere but my laptop. My day-to-day tooling is a set of Excel workbooks I maintain for myself: a position and carry tracker, a venue-cap checker and a PnL attribution sheet that I fill in by hand each evening. I would like to be the person who turns those into proper systems and I am realistic that this means learning a lot of engineering from people who do it properly.

I studied Economics at the University of Marlow Vale (BSc, 2:1, 2013 to 2016) and spent three years in an operations role at an energy trading company before moving to markets. I am a clear and calm communicator, used to explaining a position and its risk to a risk manager in two sentences, and I am happy to relocate or to work fully remote with an India-based desk.
